# Shape rules

Put the rock's line and the forecast's line on the same graph. The space between them is a shape. That shape becomes embroidery.

## Finding the shapes

1. Take one measurement at a time.
2. Start a new shape each time the two lines cross.
3. Scale each measurement by its own biggest gap, so they all fit together.
4. Shapes too thin to stitch are dropped.

## Placing the shapes: Bloom

1. The forecast is a circle. It's sewn as a triple running stitch.
2. The day runs once around the circle, clockwise, with midnight at the top. The last hour, 11 pm to midnight, stays empty.
3. A shape grows from the circle at the hour it began.
4. Rock above forecast grows **outward**. Rock below forecast grows **inward**.

| Length of the disagreement | Form |
|---|---|
| 6 hours or less | A **petal**. The longer it lasted, the longer the petal. |
| More than 6 hours | A **band** wrapped around the circle at its real hours, as wide as the gap. |

## Stitching

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Size | 90 mm across at most, for a 4 × 4 in hoop |
| Fill | Rows 0.45 mm apart |
| Longest stitch | 3 mm |
| Outline | One running stitch around each shape |
| Colours | One thread per measurement, plus one for the forecast |
| Fill angle | Each measurement has its own, and it flips above and below the forecast |

## Output

Each day makes a `.pes` file and a `.dst` file for the machine.
